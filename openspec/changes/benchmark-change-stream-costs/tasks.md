## 2. Build controlled measurement infrastructure

- [ ] 2.1 Add benchmark configuration, seeded BSON generators, versioned JSON schema, and a report validator; verify incomplete identity, environment, workload, or limitation data is rejected.
- [ ] 2.2 Add isolated replica-set setup, resource limits, a dedicated benchmark client, and optional direct-path proxy; verify unavailable Docker, cgroup/runtime CPU evidence, TLS, compression, discovery, or shared-connection conditions fail clearly.
- [ ] 2.3 Record calibration-noise uncertainty identically for every calibration sample, initial or later — half the selected round trip plus one millisecond of BSON Date quantization allowance (both `hello.localTime` and event `wallTime` are millisecond-quantized) — a low round trip is not itself a failure condition, since the dedicated local benchmark topology commonly produces round trips at or below this resolution and a low round trip is the best-case calibration scenario; the allowance simply ensures quantization-dominated error is not understated as jitter, for any sample in the sequence. Implement clock-offset estimation against the replica set's current primary (the stream's actual event source, since a change event's `wallTime` comes from whichever server originated it) per `stream-cost-benchmarking`'s formula (`offset = T_s − (t0 + t1) / 2` from the minimum-round-trip-time `hello.localTime` sample against that primary, corrected lag `= (A − W) + offset`), with `t0`, `t1`, and `A` all read from the benchmark host's wall clock — never a monotonic clock, which has no meaningful relationship to the server's wall-clock `wallTime` even though monotonic time is correctly used elsewhere in this benchmark for same-host elapsed-duration measurements — computed once per matched control/loaded pair and reused for both runs (never re-estimated per run); also sample a monotonic reading alongside every such wall-clock reading and compare elapsed monotonic versus elapsed wall-clock duration between paired readings, invalidating the pair if they diverge beyond a pre-registered tolerance (detects a host-side clock step/slew that primary-drift monitoring cannot see, since that only tracks the server's offset relative to the host); monitor primary identity via both the driver's topology events AND `electionId` recorded alongside every `hello` calibration sample (topology events can lag the server's actual election by up to a heartbeat interval; comparing `electionId` between consecutive samples does not) for the entire calibration-to-pair interval (from calibration through completion of both runs, not just while one run executes) and fail the entire pair's report validation on any primary change detected by either mechanism in that window, including between the two runs, rather than keep applying a now-stale offset; sample the same primary repeatedly throughout the pair at a fixed pre-registered cadence (at minimum: start, between the two runs, and end) and compute drift for every non-initial sample as the absolute difference between that sample's offset and the initial offset (never adjacent-to-adjacent, which understates cumulative gradual drift relative to the initial offset actually reused for corrections), failing the whole pair if any sample's drift-from-initial exceeds the pre-registered tolerance — never a signed difference, which could go negative and shrink uncertainty instead of accounting for a backward clock step; fix the sampling cadence itself as a pre-registered fraction of the acceptable-lag threshold (e.g. cadence period ≤ threshold / 10) to bound the exposure window — not the magnitude, which an undetected step by definition leaves unbounded — since discrete periodic sampling cannot detect a transient step-and-revert entirely between two consecutive samples no matter how drift is computed, and require every "healthy" conclusion to disclose the unverifiable assumption it depends on (no such step occurred) rather than present it as a proven bound; give each drift estimate its own combined uncertainty (the initial sample's calibration-noise uncertainty plus that later sample's own, not the initial sample's noise alone) and use the largest (drift + its own combined uncertainty) across all samples as `total_uncertainty`; apply the offset to correct the manager's raw `wallTime`-based invalidation-lag distribution (`stream-cost-observability`) before any threshold comparison, comparing `value + total_uncertainty` against the threshold, never the uncorrected value alone; verify the offset and its uncertainty are both recorded, that both runs in a pair reuse the same offset, that a primary change at any point in the interval (mid-run or between runs) fails the whole pair, that calibration samples are taken at the full pre-registered cadence (not only start/end) and each is checked for drift against the initial sample (not against its neighbor), that drift is computed as an absolute value, that a comparison against the acceptable-lag threshold uses the conservative `value + total_uncertainty` form, and that the "meaningfully worse" delta comparison ADDS twice `total_uncertainty` (never the raw largest drift value alone, and never subtracting it, and never comparing the raw delta directly) to the raw delta before comparing against the threshold — addition of the uncertainty-adjusted quantity, not subtraction or the unadjusted raw drift, since this check certifies health and must use the worst-case delta, not the best-case, or residual drift and its own measurement noise could mask a real regression as healthy. Compute a pre-registered-confidence block bootstrap confidence interval for each claimed percentile by resampling with replacement among the fixed-size capture windows (task 1.1), each window used whole as one block, drawn UNIFORMLY (never weighted by size — windows are already equal-sized by construction, and weighting size-weighted selection with full-block inclusion would double-count large blocks quadratically) — not a method assuming independent observations or defining its own block size across a distribution with no guaranteed contiguity — on top of the `1 / (1 − percentile)` sample-count floor which only guarantees the percentile is defined, not that it is a stable estimate; for the absolute threshold comparison, compute each run's own interval at the pair's target confidence level and expand it by `total_uncertainty` on both bounds before comparing against the threshold (the confidence interval and `total_uncertainty` are different sources of error — statistical versus clock-calibration — and neither substitutes for the other); for the delta comparison, compute each run's interval at the Bonferroni-adjusted, higher confidence level (target level's α halved) before summing their half-widths, since naively summing two intervals at the target level alone understates the combined interval's true joint coverage; treat a threshold or delta comparison as decisive only if its (correctly combined, for the delta) confidence interval does not straddle the compared-against value, and treat a straddling interval as a failing comparison, never as evidence of health.

## 3. Measure comparable workloads

- [ ] 3.1 Implement paired raw/cache idle, read-heavy, balanced, and write-dominant workloads over small, medium, and large BSON data; prime every cache workload/data-size variant through normal admissions and verify positive admission and hit counter deltas for each variant before sampling, identical workload parameters, correct returned data, exactly one consolidated stream across at least two cached collections in one database, and separately identified relevant and unrelated writes in that same database. Pair the consolidated-stream workload with a matched control: identical relevant-write traffic with unrelated-collection writes disabled versus the same relevant traffic with unrelated writes enabled, so any invalidation-lag increase can be attributed to the loaded delivery pipeline as a whole (server, transport, and router dispatch combined — this workload does not instrument router-internal time separately) rather than to baseline stream/network latency; generate one fixed, deterministic relevant-write schedule (explicit write-issue offsets from run start) before the pair executes and replay it identically in both runs, each write issued within a pre-registered per-write timing tolerance of its scheduled offset — never independently generated per run to a matching count/rate, since burst timing (not only aggregate volume) affects invalidation lag; also fix a pre-registered minimum unrelated-write count or rate in the workload configuration before the pair executes; verify both runs actually followed the schedule and that their observed relevant-write counts/rates fall within the pre-registered tolerance of each other (failing the pair otherwise, since mismatched relevant load alone can change lag independent of unrelated writes), and verify the loaded run's observed unrelated-write count or rate meets the pre-registered minimum specifically during the window relevant-write lag samples are collected — not merely at some point during the run, and not merely being enabled or nonzero — failing the run otherwise since unrelated writes outside the sampling window, or below the minimum, never meaningfully load the pipeline being measured. Reset each run in a pair to an equivalent starting state — freshly seeded database, empty cache, fresh stream cursor — rather than running them sequentially against shared, mutated state, so a lag difference cannot be caused by document or cache state carried over from the other run. Since that reset does not clear the replica-set server's own state (WiredTiger/OS page cache, oplog window), and both runs in a pair must run against the same topology (never a fresh topology per run, which would break the pair's single shared clock-offset calibration by giving each run a different primary), fix a pre-registered minimum number of repeated, counterbalanced pairs (e.g. at least three — a single pair cannot estimate variation between repeated executions, only within-pair sampling variance) and apply a pre-registered warm-up phase before each run's sampling window, so server-side cache warmth cannot differ systematically between the control and loaded runs. Before either run in a pair executes, fix the acceptable-lag threshold (percentile plus maximum corrected-lag value), the "meaningfully worse" comparison rule as an absolute-difference rule (never a percentage or ratio — the shared clock offset cancels out of an absolute difference between the two runs but not out of a ratio), and the clock-drift-tolerance bound in the workload configuration per `stream-cost-benchmarking`'s calibration requirement (task 2.3) — never choose or adjust any of them after observing results. Add a `find`/aggregate oversized-result workload composed of multiple documents individually well within the cache's configured max-entry limit (not a hard-coded 1 MiB — the limit is configurable per `implement-cache-core`) whose aggregate encoded size exceeds it — never a single document that alone exceeds the limit, which would only measure single-document rejection, not the many-document materialization cost this decision needs — distinct from the small/medium/large per-document sizes above, so the cost of materializing and discarding an over-budget result is actually measured rather than inferred from documents that fit — record this end-to-end cost (cursor fetch, transport, and the shipped encode call together) separately from, and never substituted for, the two encoder-invocation-only costs below; this variant is exempt from the admission/hit counter-delta priming rule below and instead requires a positive oversized-bypass counter delta (`stream-cost-observability`'s labelled oversized-bypass count), since its result is designed to be discarded rather than admitted. Since the shipped admission path performs one BSON encode of the complete result with no observable intermediate state to checkpoint, determine the crossover prefix empirically — not by summing individually pre-computed document sizes, which omits array-wrapper and envelope overhead the shipped encoder actually adds — by invoking the shipped encoder (with its real envelope wrapping) on successive candidate prefixes via a bounded search until the fully-enveloped encoded size first exceeds the max-entry size (setup work, not timed); then time the shipped encoder invoked repeatedly (a pre-registered minimum repetition count) on the identified prefix and repeatedly on the complete result, aggregating each set with a pre-registered rule (e.g. median) rather than relying on a single sample of either — never a separate incremental serializer and never cursor fetch or transport cost (paid by the caller either way, regardless of admission strategy). Before either measurement, fix the acceptable-savings threshold between the two encoder-invocation costs in the workload configuration — never choose or adjust it after observing results.

## 5. Resolve decisions gated on this evidence

- [ ] 5.1 Using the pre-registered minimum number of repeated, counterbalanced matched control/loaded consolidated-stream pairs (task 3.1) and their clock-offset-corrected invalidation-delivery-lag distributions (task 1.1, task 2.3) — certifying the pipeline healthy only if EVERY pair independently satisfies branch (a) below, never a majority or an average across pairs, since a single failing pair cannot be overridden by passing ones — which measure end-to-end delivery-pipeline responsiveness under load, not router-internal serialization time in isolation, and whose comparison uses the threshold and rule pre-registered in task 3.1, not one chosen after seeing results — this task can only defensibly resolve one of two ways: (a) only if the loaded run's lag is not "meaningfully worse" than the control's per the pre-registered rule AND BOTH the control run's and the loaded run's own absolute lag distributions meet the pre-registered acceptable-lag threshold (accounting for the recorded clock-offset uncertainty) — a flat delta alone is not sufficient (an already-saturated router serving only relevant traffic would show poor absolute lag in both runs without any delta to flag it), and checking the control run's absolute lag alone is not sufficient either (a permissive comparison rule could let the loaded run itself exceed the threshold while still counting as "not meaningfully worse" than an already-marginal control) — conclude the pipeline is not a measurable bottleneck under this workload and `implement-change-stream-coherency`'s single serial per-database router (shipped as-is by that change) stays as shipped — record this conclusion together with the disclosed, unverifiable assumption it depends on (no clock step-and-revert occurred entirely between two consecutive calibration samples during the pair), never presented as a fully proven guarantee; (b) otherwise — i.e. any case where (a)'s condition does not fully hold, whether because the delta is meaningfully worse, the control run's absolute lag fails the threshold, the loaded run's absolute lag fails the threshold, or any combination of these — this workload cannot attribute the failure to the router specifically versus server or transport load, so do not choose partitioning or backpressure directly from it — instead open a follow-up change scoped to add router-stage instrumentation (timestamps at event dequeue and at invalidation-apply, isolated from server/transport time) and make the partitioning/backpressure/neither choice there, once that instrumentation exists. Record whichever outcome was reached, and its supporting report, in this change's `design.md`, not in `implement-change-stream-coherency`'s — that change is expected to already be archived by the time this task runs, and archived changes are a closed historical record, not something later work reopens.
- [ ] 5.2 Using the oversized-result workload's full-result-versus-prefix encoder-invocation cost comparison (task 3.1) as a cheap triage signal only — not a proven bound on a real incremental strategy's cost in either direction, since a different encoding approach could use cheaper or more expensive primitives than the shipped bulk one-shot encoder invoked on a smaller input: (a) if the measured difference does not meet the pre-registered threshold, record a cost-benefit judgment (not a proof) that prototyping incremental abandonment is likely not worth the engineering investment, and `implement-cached-read-api`'s full-materialization admission (shipped as-is by that change) stays as shipped unless that judgment is later revisited; (b) otherwise, open a follow-up change scoped to build a minimal incremental-admission prototype and measure its actual cost, including its own bookkeeping overhead, against the same threshold — that prototype measurement, not this triage signal, is what actually decides whether to ship it. Record whichever outcome was reached, and its supporting report, in this change's `design.md`, for the same archived-change reason as 5.1.
